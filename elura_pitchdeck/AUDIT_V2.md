# ELURA Pitch Deck V2 — full audit

Updated after reviewing the shared ChatGPT critique and rechecking the deck against current workspace evidence.

## Executive assessment

The core story is strong:

`person → translation → actual space → curated result`

The weak section was the commercial proof after the product story. The previous deck moved from a compelling identity-to-space narrative into unsupported demographic targeting, mixed-unit market sizing, an overcomplicated price ladder, soft operational metrics presented like traction, and a future data moat described too much like a current asset.

V2 keeps the first eight slides mostly intact and rebuilds the commercial section around explicit hypotheses and validation gates.

## Findings and corrections

### 1. Target customer was declared before it was justified

Previous claim: `Women, 27–39` was presented as the customer definition.

Problem:

- The product is not inherently women-only.
- Internal audience data is only a modest female skew, not proof of higher purchase conversion.
- Moving, living together, first ownership and room refresh were listed as persona details rather than the reason purchase intent rises.

Correction:

- Define the customer by the **home-personalization moment** first.
- Present `urban adults 25–44 in U.S./Canada/U.K.` as an **initial channel hypothesis**, not a market boundary.
- Make the causal link explicit: the moment creates new walls, clustered decisions, identity negotiation and a desire to finish the space without renovation.

### 2. Market slide mixed incompatible units

Previous funnel:

- `$55.5B` category revenue
- `6–9M` people
- `25K–100K` annual customers

Problem:

- Revenue, population and operating goals were shown as one narrowing funnel.
- The `6–9M` and `25K–100K` figures had no reproducible calculation.
- The category included products much broader than personalized framed art.
- The cited report was a wall-decor data series, while the slide could be read as direct wall-art TAM.

Correction:

- Use current 2026 Grand View Research wall-art figures as **category context only**:
  - `$61.0B` global wall-art market in 2025
  - `$19.6B` online channel in 2025
  - `70.5%` residential share in 2025
- State that the definition includes wall coverings and is not ELURA's direct TAM.
- Remove the unsupported `6–9M` and `25K–100K` claims.
- Define future SOM bottom-up: `qualified reach × paid conversion × annual spend` after observed paid data exists.

### 3. Business model mixed products with founder labor

Previous ladder: `$19 pack → $49 founder-led direction → $150–250 art`.

Problem:

- Founder involvement is a delivery method, not a durable product tier.
- Three unrelated anchors made the core revenue engine unclear.
- The deck implied a pricing architecture before willingness to pay or physical contribution margin had been tested.

Correction:

- Entry: `$19 Room Direction Pack`.
- Core commerce: `$150–250 Personalized Art`.
- Expansion: companion sets, gifts and room refreshes, pricing TBD.
- Mark every number as a hypothesis until the first 30 paid orders establish conversion, production time, refund rate and contribution margin.

### 4. Validation omitted the hardest facts

Previous validation emphasized `17.6K viewers`, `49 reports`, `166 batches` and a live prototype.

Problem:

- These prove operating capacity, not product demand.
- The slide omitted that only `2.9%` of recent viewers were in priority North American / European markets.
- Profile visits were only `0.2%` of viewers.
- There were `0 paid orders`.

Correction:

- Headline: the system runs, demand is not yet proven.
- Show the full diagnostic set together.
- Separate built infrastructure from market traction.
- Make the next gates explicit: qualified geographic reach, measurable landing intent, first 30 paid orders, and `CAC < contribution margin` before scaling.

### 5. GTM showed a flywheel before proving the first conversion

Problem:

- The previous loop was directionally correct but visually suggested a functioning repeatable engine.
- Current geographic leakage means scaling content volume or paid media would amplify the wrong audience.

Correction:

- Use a gated launch sequence:
  1. qualify the audience,
  2. prove room-application intent,
  3. prove willingness to pay,
  4. amplify only after unit economics.
- Keep the customer-story flywheel as the output after installation, not as a current traction claim.

### 6. Defensibility described a future moat in the present tense

Problem:

- ELURA does not yet have paid selection, installed-result, satisfaction or referral data.
- `Translation data` is a potential moat, not a present proprietary asset at meaningful scale.

Correction:

- Split the slide into `What exists now` and `What compounds after paid orders`.
- Label the outcome-data system a roadmap to defensibility.

### 7. Investor completeness

Problem:

- The deck ended without a founder / current-ask slide.
- Investors could not see who built the system or what help is useful now.

Correction:

- Add a transparent founder slide: solo founder-builder across product, brand, content, analytics and QA.
- Ask for founding-customer, creator, fulfillment and proof-oriented investor introductions rather than inventing a fundraising amount.

## Slides intentionally retained

- Cover
- Problem
- Why it stays unfinished
- Why now
- Solution
- Core experience
- Competition
- Roadmap
- Closing visual

These remain consistent with the corrected commercial logic. Insight and Result are regenerated for a cleaner and less generic visual explanation.

## Truth standard for V2

- Pre-revenue.
- Zero paid orders as of the latest internal evidence.
- Audience reach is not customer traction.
- Pricing is a test hypothesis.
- Initial segment is a GTM hypothesis, not a product limit.
- Category size is context, not direct TAM.
- Data moat is prospective until paid real-world outcomes accumulate.
