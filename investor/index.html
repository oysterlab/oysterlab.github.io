The thesis

People are not only buying decor. They are trying to make their current life visible at home.

The mass art market is built around style, size, room, and color. Oysterlab starts one layer earlier: what does the user need to keep close right now?

The output is not a random poster. It is a personal art direction, original image, in-room preview, and story that explains why it belongs there now.

Service model

Self-discovery becomes a visual center.

01

Notice

A short mood-based intake asks what feeling should stay visible: focus, courage, calm, reset, warmth, beginning, keep going.

02

Translate

That answer becomes a custom art direction: color, weight, texture, movement, silence, edge, and role.

03

Place

The artwork is previewed inside the user's actual space so the room becomes the place where the feeling returns.

04

Keep

The user receives a keepsake story card, recommended size, frame direction, and print-ready output in higher tiers.

Market entry

Start where home decor already has attention, then move the frame from style to self.

Target user

27-44 urban renters, first-home owners, remote workers, and gift buyers who save home decor but want something more personal than another print.

Geography

English-speaking affluent small-space markets: Singapore, US, Australia, Canada, UK, and New Zealand. Current early signal is Singapore-skewed.

Wedge

Small daily-life spaces: bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, corridor, laundry corner, desk wall, dining nook. Not only living rooms.

Competitive lesson

Saatchi and Tappan prove quiz/advisory demand. Oysterlab's difference is the reading mechanism plus personal space placement.
